Static mock-ups for EPC-011: a Product Lead adds a roadmap from the roadmap list (STY-041, journeys 1 and 2) and adds items to a roadmap (STY-046, journeys 3 and 4). Saturday 14 November 2026. Sam Evans, who leads Outpatient Booking, is starting the Community Nursing roadmap. Five roadmaps exist, one not published.

Every design for adding a roadmap writes the same thing, at once: a new entry in the roadmap list, not published, and an empty roadmap ready for items. Every design for adding an item keeps the new item pending until Sam publishes or saves. The designs differ in where the form is, how much it asks, and where Sam is afterwards. Copy is draft, in English only.

1. Start adding a roadmap — where the form is, and how much it asks

10:40. Sam has chosen “Add a roadmap” and typed what the form asks. Nothing is saved yet.

A asks for the title alone and leaves the rest to the roadmap's own page. B asks for the essentials in a dialog. C is a page of its own with everything “About this roadmap” holds, and a preview of the card.

2. Just after adding — where Sam is, and what comes next

10:42. The roadmap was added at 10:41 with its title alone (as design A above sends it), so these frames show the emptiest case. Six roadmaps, two not published.

A stays on the list and points at the new card. B opens the new roadmap, with About in a bordered panel and “No items yet” in a dashed one. C opens it with the steps left to do. The editor has no way to add an item yet: C's “Add an item” is journeys 3 and 4 below (STY-046).

3. Add an item — where the form is, and how much it asks

11:10, on the Outpatient Booking roadmap, which is published. Sam added “Check in from your phone on arrival” to Now at 11:05; it is pending, tagged New, and counted as the one unpublished change. Sam is now adding “Choose a clinic closer to home” to Next. Nothing is public until Sam publishes.

A opens the item form as a card in the horizon it is added to. B is one button and a dialog, with the horizon as a field. C is a line under each heading: a title and Enter adds a card, and the rest is written with Edit.
